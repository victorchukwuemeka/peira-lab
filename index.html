/ methodology

How research becomes product

01

Find a real problem. The boring, repeated, money- or freedom-losing step that runs entire industries.

02

Study it on the ground. We walk the business, the network, the field — we don't guess from a questionnaire.

03

Port to the problem. The stack follows the solution — Rust for systems, TypeScript for interfaces, Python for data, hardware where physics matters, protocols where trust matters.

04

Ship and maintain. Compile it, run it, keep it alive. A lab product that isn't maintained stops working.

/ thread

One research thread, many labs

The lab has one obsession: systems that work where the world actually lives — the counter of a dry cleaner, the inside of a pharmacy shelf, the network layer of a blockchain. There is no house style, no forced stack. Offline-first when the problem has no signal. Protocol work when the problem is trust. Hardware when the problem is physics. Everything is ported to what the problem and the solution demand.

That thread runs from a Solana light node on a devnet to a dry cleaner's counter in Lagos. Nigeria has roughly 40 million businesses; fewer than 5% use modern software. Most run on notebooks, WhatsApp and memory. So half the lab studies how those businesses work, and ships tools for them — offline where there's no signal, cloud where there is, WhatsApp where that's already the workflow. The other half studies infrastructure — networks, operating systems, hardware, protocols — so the tools never depend on a technology we're not willing to build.

Payments, laundries, pharmacies, furniture, gas cylinders, phone books of 41 million people — same question everywhere: what happens from the moment this enters, until money comes out?
